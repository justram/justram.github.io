I'm Jheng-Hong (Matt) Yang. I'm exploring how agents use memory: when an agent returns to a task, what does it need to remember?

I left Mixedbread at the end of October. As a Member of Technical Staff there, I designed and built a reinforcement learning environment. We used it to train [Toast 1](https://www.mixedbread.com/blog/toast-1). I also handled evaluation and improved the harness, the software that runs the agent.

One question behind my work on search is how language models find evidence, examine it, and reason about what they find. That question led to <a href="https://github.com/justram/pi-serini"><span class="small-caps">Pi-Serini</span></a>, a minimal search agent for building reliable deep-research workflows that keep costs down.

My interest in search goes back to my doctoral research in computer science at the University of Waterloo in Canada. I studied information retrieval: how neural networks rank results, how people search through conversation, and how search works across different kinds of media. Before Waterloo, I studied recommender systems and data-driven modeling at Academia Sinica. I came to this work from physics and electronics engineering: after earning a B.S. in Electrophysics and an M.Sc. in Electronics Engineering at National Chiao Tung University, I modeled semiconductor devices in TSMC R&D.

I like building systems that stay fast and secure as more people use them. I care about whether people enjoy using them, but also about the choices we make as designers and who benefits from those choices.

If you're building a search agent or deciding how to evaluate one, I'd be glad to talk. For consulting or collaboration, on that or another problem, email me at [jhyang@stencilzeit.com](mailto:jhyang@stencilzeit.com).
