I'm Jheng-Hong (Matt) Yang. I'm exploring how a search agent can pick up where it left off: what evidence should it keep, and what does it need to look up again? I came to that question through my work on search and evaluation. At Mixedbread, I designed and built the reinforcement learning environment our team used to train [Toast 1](https://www.mixedbread.com/blog/toast-1).

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Read more about the agents I build and test</summary>

Before an agent can decide what to remember, it has to find evidence worth keeping. My work on how language models search, inspect evidence, and reason led to <a href="https://github.com/justram/pi-serini"><span class="small-caps">Pi-Serini</span></a>, a minimal search agent for building reliable deep-research workflows without letting costs run away.

As a Member of Technical Staff at Mixedbread, my work went beyond the training environment. I also handled evaluation and improved the harness, the software that runs the agent. I left Mixedbread at the end of October.

I came to agents through information retrieval. During my doctoral research in computer science at the University of Waterloo in Canada, I studied neural ranking, conversational search, and multimodal retrieval. Before Waterloo, I researched recommender systems and data-driven modeling at Academia Sinica. I started out in physics and electronics engineering: after my B.S. in Electrophysics and M.Sc. in Electronics Engineering at National Chiao Tung University, I modeled semiconductor devices in TSMC R&D.

</details>
